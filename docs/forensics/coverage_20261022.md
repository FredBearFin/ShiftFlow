# Coverage forensics (WP-2.3a), 2026-10-22

Question: on flat demand the generator builds about the right number of lines, but 20% to 24% of staff-hours land on the wrong hours. Why?

Method: read-only. The solver was not changed. Numbers come from `backend/app/tests/test_coverage_fidelity.py` (exact values, re-run by the test suite) and two throwaway scripts described under "Experiments".

## How the generator places shifts (read from `isd_rwsp_solver.py`)

1. `_solve_daily_shift_counts` (line 254): for each weekday and shift type it takes the 0.65 quantile of the demand over the hours that shift covers (a value chosen by the author, no source), then nudges each count by one up or down for two passes to lower a weighted under/over-staffing cost. Output: a target number of shifts starting on each (weekday, shift type).
2. `_partition_into_alternating_sequences` (line 311): picks the number of lines `e` from the total shift count and a target work ratio (4.65/7 at zero leeway), then splits the `7e` slots into `k` pairs of (work-stretch length, rest length). Work lengths are as equal as possible, rest lengths are as equal as possible.
3. `_assign_forward_rotating_shifts_to_work_sequences` (line 389): walks the pairs in order. Each work stretch starts on a weekday (`current_day`), and the days of a stretch must go forward M, then E, then N. For each day it picks the allowed shift code with the most remaining target (`rem_pool`) plus a bonus for matching the stage of the stretch. The remaining count can go negative: nothing stops a code being used past its target.
4. `solve()` (line 618 onward): builds the arc graph, orders the arcs with an Eulerian walk, and lays the arcs end to end. `g_start` is the start weekday of the first arc in that walk. Slot `idx` of the walk goes to position `(g_start + idx) mod 7e`, which fixes the weekday of every shift. The seven-slot rows of that array become the roster lines.

## Findings

Targets are right. On flat demand of 2 to 6 per hour, for both 8-hour M/E/N and 12-hour D/N, the target counts alone give exactly zero understaffed and zero overstaffed hours (test `test_targets_alone_leave_no_gap_on_flat_demand`). So hypothesis (ii) "targets themselves are wrong" is false for flat demand.

Placement is wrong. Flat demand 4, 8-hour M/E/N, 18 lines:

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| target M | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| actual M | 2 | 7 | 6 | 6 | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| target E | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| actual E | 2 | 7 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| target N | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| actual N | 2 | 1 | 1 | 3 | 7 | 5 | 1 |

Cause: the work stretches pile up on one start weekday. In that run, 17 work stretches begin on weekdays [Mon 2, Tue 10, Wed 1, Thu 1, Fri 1, Sat 1, Sun 1]. Once the early stretches are placed, almost every later stretch has length 5 plus rest 2 = 7 days, so it starts on the same weekday as the previous one. Every stretch must open with M (or E) and finish with N, so the opening codes land on one weekday and the nights land three to four days later. The greedy picker cannot repair that because it only chooses codes inside a stretch whose start day is already fixed.

Verdict: (i) is true in the sense that targets are right and placement is wrong, but the cause is the start-day clumping of stretches, not a missing choice of rotation offsets.

## Is the "rotate each line" idea viable?

Test: coordinate descent over the 7 rotation offsets of each output line, keeping the best (`rotation_descent` in the test file, run on the output grid, no solver change).

| Case | Gap now (under+over hours) | After per-line rotation |
|---|---|---|
| 8h M/E/N flat 2 | 208 | 48 |
| 8h M/E/N flat 4 | 320 | 128 |
| 8h M/E/N flat 6 | 352 | 160 |
| 12h D/N flat 2 | 240 | 96 |
| 12h D/N flat 4 | 264 | 240 |
| 12h D/N flat 6 | 552 | 432 |

This is an optimistic bound and not a usable fix. In the rotating roster a person moves to the next line each week, so the lines concatenated in order are the person's real sequence. Rotating one line on its own cuts that sequence: a work stretch or a rest block that spans two lines is split, and the rest hours between the end of one line and the start of the next change. The rest rule, the maximum work stretch and the forward rotation could all be broken. So the hypothesis "offsets are free parameters" is false: only a shift of the whole cycle is free, and that only changes which weekday the cycle starts on.

## Experiment: reorder the (work, rest) pairs

Throwaway subclass that rotates the list of pairs before step 3 (30 rotations tried per case, all still run through the solver's own rest checks):

| Case | Base (under/over) | Best rotation | Total ergo, base to best |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8h M/E/N flat 2 | 104/104 | 64/64 | 28 to 24 |
| 8h M/E/N flat 4 | 160/160 | 136/136 | 56 to 28 |
| 8h M/E/N flat 6 | 176/176 | 128/128 | 92 to 52 |
| 12h D/N flat 2 | 120/120 | 108/108 | 100 to 96 |
| 12h D/N flat 4 | 132/132 | 108/108 | 208 to 196 |
| 12h D/N flat 6 | 276/276 | 276/276 | 308 to 280 |

Changing only the order of pairs recovers 5% to 38% of the gap and often lowers the strain score too. It keeps the whole cycle valid, because every pair order passes through the same placement and rest checks. It does not get close to zero.

## Recommendation (for the owner to approve, amend or reject)

Do not rotate lines. Two candidate fixes, in order of safety:

1. Multi-start placement (smallest change). In `solve()`, try each cyclic rotation of the pair list (at most `e`, capped at 30) through the existing placement step, and keep the result with the lowest understaffed plus overstaffed hours, breaking ties by lower total strain, then by the lower rotation index so results stay deterministic. Expected effect: the 5% to 38% above; golden snapshots change. Risk: more solves per request (up to 30x for the placement step only, which is cheap); a lower coverage gap could raise the strain score for some presets, so WP-2.3b must show a before/after table.
2. Spread the stretch start days (larger change). Choose work and rest lengths so that the start weekdays of stretches are spread over the week, for example by alternating a few 6-day cycle pairs against 8-day ones. Higher potential gain, but it changes `_partition_into_alternating_sequences` and the work/rest balance, so it needs its own design and review.

Neither fix will reach the 10% understaffing target in the plan (WP-2.3b step 5) on every case; the real figure must be measured and stated, not assumed.

## Reproduce

`cd backend && python -m unittest app.tests.test_coverage_fidelity`. The pair-rotation experiment is a ten-line subclass overriding `_partition_into_alternating_sequences` to return `pairs[r:] + pairs[:r]`; it is not committed because it is exploratory.
