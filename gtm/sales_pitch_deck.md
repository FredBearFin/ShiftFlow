# ShiftFlow: The Owner Upgrade Deck (Secondary-Funnel Pitch Outline)

**Context — when this deck gets used at all.** ShiftFlow's primary motion is self-serve: an individual shift manager at a convenience store, liquor store, smoke shop, bar, or restaurant signs up on their own for $9–15/month, with no sales process. There is no cold outbound deck for that motion — the marketing copy in `launch_marketing_copy.md` carries it.

This deck exists for the one point in the funnel where a short pitch is useful: **after a manager has already been using ShiftFlow and has a real labor-savings number from the Labor Analysis view**, this outline is what a manager (or, occasionally, a founder on a quick call) uses to get the business owner to start paying for a business account at a higher tier (see Slide 8 for what that actually involves today — there is no automatic billing hand-off yet, even though real data/access continuity now exists via the owner-invite feature). It is an upgrade pitch to an already-warm, proven lead — not a cold enterprise sales deck, and it should never be used as one.

## Slide 1: Title
* **Headline**: ShiftFlow for {{business_name}}
* **Sub-headline**: Your manager has already been using this. Here's what it's saved you.
* **Presenter**: {{manager_name}} (or ShiftFlow, on a short intro call)

## Slide 2: What's Already Happening Here
* {{manager_name}} has been using ShiftFlow for {{duration}} to build the schedule.
* It's a personal tool — {{manager_name}} has been paying for it out of pocket.
* This isn't a pitch for something new. It's a proposal to take over paying for something that's already working.

## Slide 3: The Problem It Solved
* Building the schedule by hand for a {{business_type}} (convenience store / liquor store / smoke shop / bar / restaurant) with odd or extended hours takes real time every week — estimated at 2–3 hours for most managers — and it's hard to keep fair without software, which shows up as complaints and, eventually, turnover.
* We are not pitching this on legal risk or compliance. Most predictable-scheduling and Fair Workweek laws only apply to large chains (30+ locations); that's not the case here, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.
* The case is simpler and more honest: time saved, fewer scheduling mistakes, and a fairer rotation for the crew.

## Slide 4: The Number That Matters
* Labor Analysis for {{business_name}} shows an estimated {{labor_savings_amount}} for the current published week in {{overtime_or_extra_hours}} avoided, compared to the tightest, least-rest-optimized version of that same week's schedule ShiftFlow could have built.
* This is pulled directly from the app's own Labor Analysis view, via its "Copy summary for owner" button — it's {{business_name}}'s own schedule data, not a projection. It is **not** a comparison against the manager's actual pre-ShiftFlow schedules; ShiftFlow doesn't keep a record of what was used before it, so "compared to before" should not be the framing used with the owner.

## Slide 5: What Changes on the Business Tier
* **Personal tier (what {{manager_name}} is on today)**: $9–15/month, one manager, billed personally.
* **Business tier (the proposal)**: roughly $20–50/month per location, billed to the business.
* **What's actually built today**: the Business tier is still the same checkout/account type as Personal at signup — it's a higher price, not a different billing mechanism yet. A Business-tier signup does not automatically take over {{manager_name}}'s existing account or billing.
* **What is built, and is the real continuity story today**: {{manager_name}} can use the in-app *Invite an owner* feature to give the owner their own separate login, linked to this facility. Once {{owner_name}} accepts, they get a fast labor-cost summary *and* can open the same scheduling tool {{manager_name}} uses for this location — the real roster, labor analysis, coverage, ergonomics, and the ability to run new schedules, add staff, or make edits themselves. That login, and everything it can see and do, keeps working even if {{manager_name}}'s login is later deleted — real continuity, and it is no longer visibility-only. What it still doesn't do is move billing: the owner's access is additive on top of the existing facility, not a transfer of who pays for it.

## Slide 6: Why This Matters Beyond the Dollar Number
* Shift-manager turnover in retail and food service is reported industry-wide in roughly the 28–47%/year range depending on the segment — high enough that most locations will eventually lose the manager who set this up.
* A personal account disappears when the manager does. A business account is *meant* to not disappear with them — that continuity, not a new feature, is most of what the business tier is pitched on. The piece of that which is real today is the owner-invite login (see Slide 5): the data survives, and so does the owner's ability to actually run the schedule with it, not just look at a number. What's still on the roadmap is the billing/ownership hand-off itself — don't promise that part as already live.

## Slide 7: What This Pitch Is Not
* We're not asking you to switch away from a competitor — most businesses this size are scheduling on paper, a group chat, or a spreadsheet, not on Homebase, 7shifts, or Deputy. The honest comparison is "the way you do it now," not a rival product.
* We're not pitching enterprise features, API integrations, or multi-location rollups unless {{business_name}} actually operates more than one location.

## Slide 8: The Ask
* There is no one-click "move billing" action today. Concretely, the Business-tier ask is: the owner signs up for ShiftFlow's Business plan themselves, on the pricing page, as its own account. That new account does **not** automatically inherit {{manager_name}}'s existing schedule, crew list, or history — it starts from scratch, exactly like any other new signup. Say this plainly rather than implying a seamless handoff.
* Separately, and available right now at no extra cost: the *Invite an owner* feature already in the app gives {{owner_name}} their own login into {{manager_name}}'s real facility — not a copy, the same data. {{manager_name}} invites them by email, {{owner_name}} signs in separately, and from there they can see the labor-cost summary *or* open the full scheduling tool for this location (roster, staff, labor analysis, coverage, ergonomics, new solves) and act on it, while {{manager_name}} keeps scheduling exactly the same way alongside them. This is the thing to lead with when the owner just wants real visibility and the ability to step in, not a billing change.
* No contract on either tier; it's a monthly subscription, cancel anytime.

## Slide 9: Proof Points to Leave Behind
* A one-page printout of the Labor Analysis savings number.
* The current schedule, so the owner can see what it actually looks like.
* {{manager_name}}'s own account of what it replaced (time, or specific scheduling disputes it resolved).
